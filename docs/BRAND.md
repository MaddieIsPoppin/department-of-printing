# Department of Printing: brand foundation

## Business and positioning

Department of Printing is a South African custom garment printing business. Customers choose existing blank garments sourced from suppliers and personalise them with printed artwork. The business does not manufacture custom garments.

The initial range will include T-shirts, hoodies and sweaters, with other printable garments considered later. Position the offering as affordable custom clothing with strong visual quality: "This looks really good — and the prices are surprisingly accessible."

## Personality and target experience

Be confident, creative, approachable and precise. Bring modern streetwear sensibility together with editorial and graphic-design influence, with a slightly industrial/technical character. Make personal expression feel accessible to a first-time customer.

Customers should understand what they can print, what they are buying and what it costs. Shopping and customisation should feel calm and easy even when the underlying production process is complex. Show useful prices clearly once real pricing is available; do not invent prices or business promises for visual effect.

## What the brand should and should not feel like

- Feel clean, modern, considered and easy on the eye.
- Feel creatively expressive through garments and customer artwork.
- Avoid luxury signalling or an intimidatingly expensive impression.
- Avoid corporate styling, discount-site tactics and generic storefront templates.
- Avoid excessive colour, over-design and formulaic AI-generated landing-page patterns.

## Current visual direction

The direction is exploratory, not an approved final identity. Start with charcoal, off-black, grey and off-white, strong typography, intentional negative space and garment-led imagery. Explore subtle grids and geometric details where they help organise information. Let garments and artwork supply most of the colour.

Existing brand work pairs large condensed typography with smaller technical typography. Preserve that reference when exploring designs without committing to specific fonts or a fixed design system yet.

## Brand language

The brand concept/tagline is **"WEAR WHAT YOU CAN IMAGINE."**

Use clear, direct, welcoming language and South African English, including "colour" and "customise". Describe the service as printing on existing garments. Avoid language suggesting bespoke garment manufacturing, such as "we manufacture your design".

Useful action language includes "Choose your garment", "Add your artwork" and "Choose print locations". Explain technical printing terms when they affect a customer's choice. Use "Print Lab" for the future garment designer, with a plain-language explanation for new users. Avoid unsupported quality, turnaround, sustainability or price claims.
